    Guide

    How Signdex works

    Signdex is a dictionary for single ASL signs. You show it one sign, from a video or your webcam, and it suggests the five signs it knows that move most like it. It does not interpret or translate sentences, and everything that looks at your video runs inside your browser.

    1 Show it a sign

    • Webcam: press Record (or Space / R), wait for 3, 2, 1, then sign one sign within the 2 seconds. Press again to try another.
    • Uploaded clip: drag the box on the timeline around the sign (1 to 3 seconds; arrow keys work too) and choose Look up.
    • Chrome extension: while a video plays, press Alt+Shift+S to start, then press it again right after the sign to look up the last 1 to 5 seconds.

    2 Track hands, face and body

    Google’s MediaPipe models find 543 landmark points in each video frame, about 20 times a second: 21 on each hand, 468 on the face and 33 on the body. Signdex keeps the 109 that matter for signing: both hands, the lips, 20 points around the eyes, eyebrows and face outline, and 7 upper-body points (nose, shoulders, elbows, wrists). Turn on “Show hand & body points” to see what it tracks.

    3 Prepare the movement

    • Frames before the first and after the last visible hand are dropped.
    • Left-handed signing is mirrored, so it’s compared like right-handed signing.
    • Positions are centered between the shoulders and scaled by shoulder width, so distance from the camera and where you stand don’t matter.
    • The picture shape is corrected to match the videos the model learned from.
    • The sign is resampled to 64 frames, so fast and slow signing look alike.

    4 Recognize the sign

    A small neural network (convolution and attention layers) compares the movement with the 250 signs it was trained on. It learned from about 77,000 recordings of the PopSign ASL dataset. On signers it never saw during training it puts the right sign first 69% of the time and in the top 5 about 84% of the time. Each lookup also checks a few slightly trimmed versions of your clip and averages them, and takes about a fifth of a second on a laptop.

    5 Read the results

    • The five most likely signs, with a confidence bar: high (60% or more), medium (40 to 60%) or low. Below 40% for the top match, Signdex says it isn’t sure.
    • A small animation of a PopSign signer making each sign, drawn as a skeleton and mirrored like your webcam view.
    • “Looks similar to”: signs the model most often mixes up with this one, worth comparing.
    • “Watch a real signer” opens the sign in an online ASL dictionary, the best way to check a match.

    6 Learn and practice

    Save signs to My signs (stored on this device). With four or more saved, start a quiz: multiple-choice questions about your signs, written by Signdex’s quiz service. If it can’t be reached, a built-in quiz uses look-alike signs instead.

    7 Privacy

    Your camera and video are processed only in your browser; no video, image or landmark leaves your device. The only data Signdex sends is the list of your saved sign names, when you start a quiz. A “Watch a real signer” link you open takes you to that dictionary’s website.

    8 Tips for good results

    • Keep your head, shoulders and both hands in the picture, with good light from the front.
    • Sign one sign, at a normal pace, and keep the clip tight around it.
    • If the top match is unsure, try again or trim the clip closer to the sign.

    9 What it can’t do

    Signdex knows 250 common signs from the PopSign ASL vocabulary. It looks up one isolated sign at a time; it can’t follow fingerspelling, sentences or conversation, and its results are suggestions to compare, not translations.

    Privacy

    Your video stays on your device

    Signdex runs entirely in your browser. When you use your webcam or a video, the frames are turned into landmark points and matched against the model on your own device. Your video and those landmarks are never uploaded or stored. The only thing ever sent off your device is the text names of the signs you save, which are used to generate your practice quizzes.
